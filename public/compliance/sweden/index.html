The legal framework

As an EU member state, Sweden applies the GDPR to all processing of personal data — including B2B contact data — alongside its national implementation of the ePrivacy Directive, which governs electronic marketing specifically. The general EU pattern: B2B email to corporate role addresses can rest on legitimate interest with a documented balancing assessment, while individual and consumer addresses require consent; national transpositions vary in strictness (Germany and Austria apply competition-law standards that are effectively stricter). Every message needs sender identification and a working opt-out honored promptly, and suppression must persist across campaigns.

What senders must do in Sweden

  1. Identify yourself in every message — sender name, organization, and a means of contact.
  2. Provide a working opt-out and honor it promptly and permanently; suppression persists across campaigns and tools.
  3. Match the message to the role — relevance to the recipient's professional function is the load-bearing argument under most regimes, and the largest performance lever besides.
  4. Document your basis — where the data came from and why the outreach is justified under GDPR + national ePrivacy implementation; be able to answer "where did you get my email" accurately.
  5. Meet the technical layer: since 2024, Google and Yahoo require bulk senders to authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and to keep spam-report rates below 0.3%.
