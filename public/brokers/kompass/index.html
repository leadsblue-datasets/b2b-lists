The market splits into two economic models. Data platforms (ZoomInfo, Apollo, Cognism) sell continuous access: filters, integrations, refreshed records — priced as annual subscriptions that make sense for teams prospecting every day. List sellers (brokers and one-time catalogs) sell a file: you pay once, own the export, and owe nothing next month — which is the rational choice for defined campaigns, market tests, and any team without a daily outbound motion. Neither is universally better; the mistake is paying platform prices for a one-campaign job, or expecting a static file to behave like a live database. The 7-point checklist before you buy from anyone

Whichever vendor you shortlist, the same seven checks separate usable files from expensive bounces:

  1. Sample first. Any legitimate seller will provide 25–50 sample records for your exact segment. Refusal is disqualifying.
  2. Verify the sample. Run it through a verification tool. A sample returning more than ~5% invalid addresses predicts a file you'll regret.
  3. Ask the refresh date. When was this file last validated? A specific answer ("re-verified in the last 90 days") beats a vague one ("continuously updated").
  4. Check field completeness. A contact record without company, title, and a person-level (not info@) address is worth a fraction of a complete one.
  5. Confirm sourcing. Compiled from public/business registries, opt-in programs, or scraped? The answer determines both quality and your legal exposure.
  6. Get the price in writing per record, including every "select" surcharge, before committing.
  7. Review the replacement policy. Reputable sellers replace or credit hard bounces above a stated threshold.

Before you send: the workflow that protects your domain

Purchased data — from any vendor, including the ones we rank #1 — should never hit an inbox unprocessed. The workflow that protects your sending domain:

  1. Verify the full file with a verification service; remove invalid, disposable, and role addresses; treat catch-alls as a separate lower-trust segment.
  2. Authenticate your domain before the first send. Since 2024, Google and Yahoo require bulk senders to authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and to keep spam-report rates below 0.3%.
  3. Warm up. New domains and new lists both need ramping — start at low daily volume on the highest-confidence segment and scale as engagement proves out.
  4. Segment by confidence. Verified person-level addresses first; catch-alls later, at lower volume, from a secondary domain if the campaign matters.
  5. Watch the kill metrics. Hard bounces above ~2% or spam reports approaching 0.3% mean stop, re-clean, and slow down — pushing through burns the domain.

B2B contact data decays at roughly 22–30% per year as people change jobs and companies restructure, so any purchased list should be re-verified before sending.

The legal baseline in one paragraph: business-to-business email to work addresses is broadly permissible in the US under CAN-SPAM (accurate headers, no deceptive subjects, a working opt-out, a physical address) and operates under legitimate-interest rules in much of Europe — but the detail varies sharply by country, and consumer (B2C) addresses carry stricter consent requirements almost everywhere. Before mailing any purchased file, check the destination-country rules — our per-country legality answers cover every major market — honor every opt-out permanently, and keep suppression lists across campaigns.
