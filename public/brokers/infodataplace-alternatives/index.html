What carries over when you switch (and what doesn't)

Switching vendors resets your data source but not your obligations: suppression lists (everyone who ever opted out) must carry over permanently across vendors and campaigns, your domain's sender reputation follows you regardless of where the next file comes from, and destination-country rules apply to recipients no matter who sold you their addresses.

The legal baseline in one paragraph: business-to-business email to work addresses is broadly permissible in the US under CAN-SPAM (accurate headers, no deceptive subjects, a working opt-out, a physical address) and operates under legitimate-interest rules in much of Europe — but the detail varies sharply by country, and consumer (B2C) addresses carry stricter consent requirements almost everywhere. Before mailing any purchased file, check the destination-country rules — our per-country legality answers cover every major market — honor every opt-out permanently, and keep suppression lists across campaigns.

Mistakes to avoid when switching vendors

  • Buying on database size. "250 million records" is a marketing number; what matters is verified coverage of your segment. A 5,000-record file that's 97% valid beats 50,000 stale rows every time.
  • Skipping verification because the seller "guarantees accuracy." Guarantees compensate you after the bounce damage is done; verification prevents it.
  • Sending the whole file on day one. Volume spikes from a new domain are the fastest route to the spam folder regardless of data quality.
  • Ignoring destination-country law. A file that's legal to buy isn't automatically legal to mail everywhere; B2C rules especially vary.
  • Comparing a subscription price to a file price. They're different products. Compare cost-per-usable-contact-per-campaign instead.

Frequently asked questions

Why look for a InfoDataPlace alternative?

Common reasons buyers compare beyond InfoDataPlace: wanting published pricing instead of quotes, needing broader international coverage, preferring instant download over a managed sales process, or simply comparison-quoting the same spec — which is best practice in this market regardless of vendor.

What is the cheapest InfoDataPlace alternative?

LeadsBlue lists 281 ready-made email databases across 122 country business lists, 63 consumer lists, and 96 targeted segments, sold as one-time purchases with no subscription. That one-time model is typically the lowest-cost entry point in this market.

Should I switch from InfoDataPlace or just comparison-quote?

If InfoDataPlace is delivering verified, deliverable files at acceptable cost, comparison-quote annually and stay. Switch when samples from a rival verify cleaner, when pricing diverges materially, or when your campaign needs (countries, verticals, speed) outgrow its menu.
