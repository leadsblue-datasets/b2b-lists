How to Set Up Cold Email Infrastructure

BDBy B2B Data Index Research Team · Updated 2026-07-20 · Dataset v5.0 (2026-Q3)
The 6 steps: 1. Buy lookalike sending domains2. Authenticate everything on day one3. Create mailboxes conservatively4. Choose the cold-outreach platform5. Warm before volume6. Instrument the kill metrics
Quick answer

Cold email runs on infrastructure the marketing ESP world never shows you: separate domains, full authentication, warmed mailboxes, and volume discipline. The build order: (half a day setup + 2–4 weeks warm-up · for teams building an outbound sending stack.)

Step 1: Buy lookalike sending domains

Never send cold from your primary domain. Register 2–3 close variants (get-yourco.com, yourco-hq.com), set them to redirect to the main site, and accept that this insulation is the whole point: outreach experiments can't damage the domain your invoices and password resets depend on.

Step 2: Authenticate everything on day one

Per sending domain: SPF (one record, correct includes), DKIM (2048-bit, signing enabled), DMARC (start p=none with reports, ratchet later), plus MX and a plausible sending identity. Our deliverability guides cover each record's specifics and failure modes.

Step 3: Create mailboxes conservatively

2–3 mailboxes per domain, real names, profile photos, signatures. Each mailbox will carry 30–50 cold sends/day at maturity — capacity planning is mailboxes × 40, and scaling is horizontal (more mailboxes/domains), never vertical.

Step 4: Choose the cold-outreach platform

This is the Instantly/Smartlead/Woodpecker category — sequencing, inbox rotation, and reply detection built for unsolicited sending on YOUR domains. Marketing ESPs prohibit purchased lists and pool your reputation with strangers; that's the architectural reason for the separate stack, not a workaround.

Step 5: Warm before volume

2–4 weeks of ramped, engaged sending before the first real campaign — the warm-up guide covers the schedule. Load your verified, best-fit segment first when campaigns begin.

Step 6: Instrument the kill metrics

Google Postmaster Tools per domain; alarms at 0.1% spam rate and 2% hard bounces; weekly review. Infrastructure isn't set-and-forget — it's an operating practice with a dashboard.

Whatever provider you buy from, run the file through an email verification tool before the first send — a hard-bounce rate above about 2% damages sender reputation.

#

How to know it's working

Every workflow on this page resolves to measurable outcomes, and the benchmarks to measure against are published: verified B2B lists open at 18–26% (median 22%) and reply at 2–5% (median 3.2%), with hard bounces under 2% and spam reports under 0.1% as the operating bands. Landing inside those ranges means the workflow held; landing outside them points at which step slipped — high bounces indict verification, low opens indict deliverability, low replies with healthy delivery indict relevance. The statistics library carries the full ranges by country, industry, and seniority.

The rules that apply throughout

B2B contact data decays at roughly 22–30% per year as people change jobs and companies restructure, so any purchased list should be re-verified before sending. Since 2024, Google and Yahoo require bulk senders to authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC and to keep spam-report rates below 0.3%.

Frequently asked questions

How much does cold email infrastructure cost?

Domains (~$10–15/year each), mailboxes (~$6–8/month each), an outreach platform (tens of dollars monthly), verification per file — a functioning stack runs well under $150/month at starter scale, against the enterprise data contracts it replaces.

Can I just use Gmail and Mailchimp?

A personal Gmail lacks the volume ceiling and Mailchimp prohibits purchased lists outright — both are architecture mismatches, not policy technicalities. The separate stack exists because the job is different.

How long does this take?

half a day setup + 2–4 weeks warm-up. The steps are ordered by dependency — each is a precondition for the next mattering.
